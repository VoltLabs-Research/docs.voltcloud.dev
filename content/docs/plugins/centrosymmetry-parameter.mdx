---
title: Centrosymmetry Parameter
description: Measure how far each atom's neighbor shell is from centrosymmetric, to find dislocation cores, stacking faults and surfaces in centrosymmetric crystals.
---

The centrosymmetry parameter (CSP) measures how much an atom's neighbor shell departs from inversion symmetry. In a perfect centrosymmetric lattice such as FCC or BCC each neighbor has an opposite partner and the CSP is close to zero; at dislocation cores, stacking faults, surfaces and vacancies it grows. Use it to highlight defects in FCC and BCC metals, or as a feature for [MultiSOM](/docs/plugins/multisom). It is not meaningful for lattices without inversion symmetry at the atom, such as HCP or diamond.

| Key | Version | Builds |
|---|---|---|
| `centrosymmetry-parameter` | 2.0.3 | Linux, macOS, Windows |

This plugin has no browser build, so it can't be installed on a machine that runs in the browser or the desktop app.

## How it works

For each atom, the plugin takes its N nearest neighbors and pairs them up into N/2 pairs of opposite neighbors. The CSP is the sum over those pairs of |rᵢ + rⱼ|², where rᵢ and rⱼ are the bond vectors to the two neighbors of a pair. The two modes differ in how they pick the pairs:

- **Conventional** follows the original definition: it takes the N/2 smallest values of |rᵢ + rⱼ|² over all neighbor pairs.
- **Matching** finds the pairing (a minimum-weight perfect matching) that minimizes the sum, so each neighbor is used exactly once.

## Inputs

- Atom positions and the simulation cell.

## Arguments

| Argument | Type | Default | Allowed values | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Num Neighbors** `num_neighbors` | number | `12` | ≥ 2, step 1 | Number of nearest neighbors N. Use 12 for FCC and 8 for BCC. It must be even, and at most 32. |
| **Mode** `mode` | select | `conventional` | `conventional` (Conventional), `matching` (Matching) | How neighbors are paired; see above. |

## Outputs

| Exposure | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Centrosymmetry** | A listing with the frame's summary. |
| **Centrosymmetry Model** | A listing, and a 3D model of the atoms. |

The workflow declares the per-atom properties `csp` (Centrosymmetry (CSP)) and `coordination` (Coordination number).

## Notes

- The CSP has units of length squared and scales with the square of the lattice constant, so a threshold that separates defects from crystal in one material doesn't carry over to another.
- Thermal vibrations raise the CSP of crystalline atoms; compare frames at the same temperature.
